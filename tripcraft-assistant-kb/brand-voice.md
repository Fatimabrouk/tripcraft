# tripcraft — Brand Voice & Style Guide

_Use this so every launch asset sounds and looks like tripcraft, whether it's a LinkedIn post, a release note, or the announcement page._

## Who we are
tripcraft is a travel booking platform — stays, activities, and now whole trips. Our promise: **everything for the trip, in one place.** We take the friction out of travel so people spend less time planning and more time going.

## Personality
Think of tripcraft as **the well-traveled friend who happens to know every city.** Warm, effortless, quietly confident. Excited about travel but never breathless or salesy.

## Tone
- **Warm, not corporate.** Talk to a person, not a segment.
- **Effortless.** Short sentences. Say the simple thing.
- **Confident, not hypey.** We don't need exclamation marks to be excited.
- **Grounded.** Real places, real moments — not "unlock unforgettable experiences."

## Do
- Lead with the traveler's benefit ("plan your whole trip in minutes"), not the tech.
- Use plain language. "Your trip, planned" beats "AI-powered itinerary generation."
- Be specific and human — name the coffee, the neighborhood, the pace.
- Keep it tight. If a word isn't earning its place, cut it.

## Don't
- Don't over-promise. v1 is **single-city** — never imply multi-city trips.
- Don't drown it in "AI." Mention it once if useful, then talk about the trip.
- Don't use travel clichés: _bucket list, hidden gems, off the beaten path, tripcraftlust overload, unforgettable, seamless, game-changer._
- Don't guarantee prices or availability — plans are suggestions, confirmed at booking.

## Key phrases (reuse these)
- "Everything for the trip, in one place."
- "Your trip, planned in minutes."
- "Tell us the trip you want."
- "Like a friend who lives there."

## Boilerplate (about tripcraft — use at the end of posts/notes)
> tripcraft is where millions of travelers book stays, activities, and now whole trips — everything for the trip, in one place.

## Visual identity (for the announcement page)
Use these so the HTML announcement looks polished and on-brand.

**Color palette**
| Role | Color | Hex |
|------|-------|-----|
| Primary (brand) | Deep teal | `#0E7C7B` |
| Ink / text | Near-black | `#1A2A33` |
| Background | Warm sand | `#F4E9DD` |
| Surface / cards | Off-white | `#FBF9F6` |
| Accent (CTA, highlights) | Coral | `#FF6B57` |
| Muted text / borders | Warm gray | `#8A8078` |

**Typography**
- Headings: a clean modern sans-serif (e.g. system UI / Inter), bold, generous size.
- Body: same sans-serif, comfortable line height, plenty of whitespace.

**Style notes**
- Rounded corners, soft shadows, lots of breathing room — feels calm and premium.
- Coral is for the one thing you want people to do (the CTA). Use it sparingly.
- Big, warm, confident headline. One clear call to action.
